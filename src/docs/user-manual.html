User Manual

RepairCafe — repair tracking for a sustainable future — v1.24

🔊 Read aloud

Overview

RepairCafe is a web application for tracking repair items at Repair Cafe sessions. Volunteers use it to register items brought in for repair, assign repairers, record outcomes, and review how each session went.

The app is hosted online and works on any modern browser — no installation needed.

Listening to this manual

This manual can be read aloud. Use the 🔊 Read aloud bar at the top of the page; it stays in view as you scroll.

  • From the beginning — press ▶ Read to hear the whole manual.
  • A chosen section — pick it in the drop-down (From: Analytics, for example) and press ▶ Read. It carries on to the end unless you tick Just this section. Or press the 🔊 Read button next to any section heading to hear just that section.
  • ⏸ Pause / ▶ Resume and ⏹ Stop control the reading, and the speed drop-down makes it slower or faster.

The part being read is highlighted, and the page scrolls to follow it. Reading uses your browser's own voice, so nothing is sent anywhere. It works in Chrome, Edge and Safari on computers and phones. On a phone, check the volume and that the phone isn't on silent.

Logging In

Sign in with your Google account: press Continue with Google and choose your account. Google is the only way to sign in.

The first time you sign in, an admin needs to approve your account before you can see the repair records. The page opens by itself as soon as they do.

Used email/password or Facebook before? Those sign-in options have been removed. Sign in with Continue with Google instead. If Google sign-in says your email address was registered another way, ask a Repair Cafe admin to reset your account to Google.

Once logged in your name appears in the header. The Admin icon is only shown to admins. To log out, click the arrow/logout icon in the top-right corner.

How My Repairs knows who you are: the app links your sign-in to a repairer name in this order:
  1. the name you picked yourself (see below);
  2. any of the email addresses on your repairer record;
  3. your Google name matching your repairer name exactly.
“Which repairer are you?” If the app can't tell (for example you sign in with a different email address, or your repairer name is “Bob” but your Google name is “Robert Smith”), clicking My Repairs asks Which repairer are you? Choose your name from the list and press This is me. The app remembers it for your sign-in, so you only do this once.

Wrong person, or no items? At the top of My Repairs it says Showing repairs for …. Click Not you? Change and pick the name your repairs are recorded under.

Taken Home items: set an item's status to Taken Home when a volunteer takes it away between sessions. It then leaves the Active list but stays tracked under Taken Home. When it comes back, change its status (for example to Investigating, or use Complete) and it moves out of the Taken Home list automatically.

Electrical items show a ⚡ PAT badge before the description: ⚡ PAT ✓ passed, ⚡ PAT ✗ failed, or ⚡ PAT ? not tested yet. On a computer, hover over the badge to see who tested it and when; the item's details page shows this too.

To list items by PAT result, click ⚲ Filters and use the ⚡ PAT drop-down next to the description filter: All electrical, PAT passed, PAT failed or PAT not tested. It works together with the buttons above — choose All to search every session rather than just the latest.

Repair Statuses

Each item moves through these steps, in this order:

1. New → 2. Investigating → 3. Taken Home (only if needed) → 4. Completed → 5. Collected

StatusMeaning
NewItem registered, no repairer working on it yet
InvestigatingA repairer is looking at the item. Choosing a repairer sets this automatically.
Taken HomeA volunteer has taken the item home to keep working on it between Repair Cafe sessions. These items are not counted as active work for a session; they are listed under the Taken Home button at the top of the repair list (with a count), whichever session they came from, so they are not forgotten.
CompletedThe repair work is finished. Every completed item also records how it went (see below).
CollectedThe owner has taken the item away. How the repair went is still kept and shown, e.g. Collected · Repaired.

How the repair went (when Completed)

OutcomeMeaning
RepairedItem successfully fixed
Partially RepairedSome repair was carried out but the item is not fully fixed
UnrepairableThe item could not be fixed
Advice GivenNo repair was made, but the owner was given advice

Completing a repair

  1. Open the item and press Complete (or choose Complete from the ⋯ menu in the repair list).
  2. Choose how the repair went: Repaired, Partially Repaired, Unrepairable or Advice Given.
  3. Answer Has the owner collected the item? — usually Yes at a session. Choose Not yet if the item is waiting to be picked up.
  4. For electrical items, fill in the ⚡ PAT Test: the name of the PAT tester and whether it Passed or Failed (leave it on Not tested yet if it hasn't been done).
  5. Record the owner's satisfaction, any donation and notes, then press Mark as Complete.

PAT tests (electrical items)

Every item in the Electrical category has a ⚡ PAT Test section showing who tested it, whether it passed or failed, and the date the result was recorded. You can fill it in on the Complete screen or in the PAT Test section of the Edit screen, and it is shown on the item's details page. Start typing the tester's name to pick from the list of repairers, or type any name.

Where to see PAT results:

  • Item details page — result, tester and date
  • Repair list — a ⚡ PAT ✓ / ✗ / ? badge on each electrical item, and a ⚡ PAT filter under ⚲ Filters (see Repair List)
  • Session dashboard — a ⚡ PAT Tests card counting passed, failed and not tested, a PAT column in the items list, and a PAT line for each electrical item in the session report (see Session Dashboard)
  • Analytics (see Analytics) — a ⚡ PAT Tests per Session chart with a bar for each session (passed in green, failed in red, not tested in grey), plus totals and an overall pass rate. Use the year buttons to look at one year. It only appears on Live App Data and only counts electrical items entered in the app, because items imported from old spreadsheets have no PAT details. The pass rate is worked out from the items that were actually tested.
Failed PAT test: an item that fails must not be handed back for use until it has been made safe.

If an item was completed but not yet collected, open it later and press 👋 Owner has collected it when the owner picks it up.

You can also change the status at any time from the Status dropdown in the repair list or on the Edit screen.

AI Repair Suggestions

The AI helper can suggest what might be wrong with an item and how to fix it. It's best kept for tricky faults — most repairs don't need it. There are two ways to open it:

  • On the Edit Repair Item screen, under the Fault box: Stuck on a tricky fault? 🤖 Get repair ideas. Any changes you've made are saved first, so the AI sees the latest fault.
  • From the ⋯ menu next to an item in the repair list, choose 🤖 Repair ideas (AI).

Check saved answers first

Every AI answer is saved with its item, so before you ask the AI it's worth seeing if a similar item has already been answered. The repair ideas page shows:

  • 📌 Already asked for this item — the answer (and any follow-ups) from an earlier visit.
  • 💡 Similar items with a saved AI answer — up to three items whose description, fault, make or model match this one.
  • A search box — search every saved answer by item, make, model, fault or anything in the answer, for example kettle, Dyson or no power.

Press Show to read a saved answer. If it fits, press Use this answer for this item: it is copied to this item (noting which item it came from) without asking the AI again, and you can still ask follow-up questions. If none of them help, press Ask AI for a fresh answer.

Why check first? Searching saved answers is instant and free; each Ask AI is a new request to the AI service.

What is sent to the AI

  • The item's description, the fault, and any other details recorded (make, model, colour and so on)
  • All photos already on the item — including those added when the item was booked in, on the Edit screen, or imported from the spreadsheet — plus any you add on this screen

The AI looks at the photos and says what it can see, so a clear picture of the fault or the rating label often helps a lot.

Photo limit: up to the 6 most recent photos are sent with each question. If an item has more, add the most useful close-up last.

Asking follow-up questions

After the first answer you can type a follow-up question. Use the camera or file button beside the question box to add a new photo first — it is saved to the item and sent with your question, together with the item's other photos.

Saving the AI response

Press Save AI Response to save the answer as a report you can open or send to the owner by WhatsApp. The button is at the top of the AI card and again at the bottom, so you don't need to scroll back up after reading. Once saved, an Open report link appears beside it.

A report includes the follow-up questions asked so far. If you ask more afterwards, press Save AI Response again to include them.

Analytics

The analytics dashboard shows how the Repair Café is doing over time: how many items come in, what happens to them, and how many people visit. Open it with the Analytics button (bar chart icon) in the header.

Where the figures come from

Two buttons at the top choose the data:

On Live App Data, each session date uses one source only, in this order: items entered in the app, then imported monthly totals, then items imported from old spreadsheets. A session is never counted twice.

Importing old figures from a spreadsheet

  1. Click Upload File, then drop your Excel file (.xlsx or .xls) onto the box, or click it to choose the file. The workbook needs sheets named Monthly_Data followed by the year, for example Monthly_Data 2025.
  2. Check the charts look right. Use 📂 New file to try a different file.
  3. Click ☁ Import to Firebase. When it says ✓ Imported, switch back to Live App Data to see the old sessions alongside the app's own records.
Importing the same file again is safe: each session date is stored once, and importing again replaces it. Dates that already have items entered in the app keep using the app's figures.

Issue Tracker

Use the issue tracker to log operational problems or improvement ideas (e.g. equipment faults, process suggestions). Access it via the Issues icon in the header — approved users only.

A count badge on the Issues button shows the number of open issues at a glance.

Reporting an issue

  1. Click Report Issue
  2. Enter a description of the issue
  3. Click Submit Issue

Talking about an issue

Each issue has its own conversation, so the person who raised it and the person fixing it can ask questions and give updates.

  1. Click the 💬 Discuss button under the issue's description (it shows the number of messages once there are some)
  2. Read the conversation — it starts with the original issue, marked raised this issue
  3. Type your reply in the box at the bottom and click Send (or press Ctrl+Enter)

Your own messages appear on the right, everyone else's on the left. A red New label on the 💬 button means someone has replied since you last opened that conversation on this device. Click the button again to close the conversation.

Finding an issue

Filters above the list help you find issues quickly:

  • Status buttons — All, Open (anything not yet fixed), New, Assigned or Fixed. Each shows how many issues it has.
  • Search — looks through the description, the fix, who raised it and the replies in the conversation.
  • Raised by — anyone, Raised by me, or a particular person.
  • Raised — in the last 7, 30 or 90 days, or More than 90 days ago. The last option helps find old issues that may have been forgotten.
  • 💬 New replies only — issues where someone else has replied since you last opened the conversation.
  • Clear resets the filters. While a filter is on, the screen shows Showing X of Y issues.

Clicking the Status column heading still sorts the list.

Making the list easier to read

Above the right-hand side of the table is a Fewer columns switch. Slide it on to hide the #, Date Raised and Actions columns — the Description and Fix columns then spread out to fill the space, so long descriptions wrap onto fewer lines. Slide it off to bring the hidden columns back.

Good to know: the switch is remembered on each device, so it stays as you left it next time. The 🗑️ delete button is in the Actions column, so turn the switch off when you need to delete an issue. Issues with no fix yet keep their orange marker on the left edge either way.

Finding a repairer: filters at the top of the list:

Database Explorer

A full searchable view of all repair records across every session. Click a row to open the full repair detail. Use the print icon on a row to generate a PDF repair receipt in a new window. Choose a collection on the left (Repair Items, Repairers, Visitors, Tags and so on) to see its records.

Finding records:

  • 🔍 Search — searches every field of every record, including the record ID, notes and outcome. Type several words to find records containing all of them.
  • ⚲ Column filters (Repair Items only) — a row of filters under the column headings for number, description, visitor, item type, repairer, session, date created and status.
  • ⚙ Field filter (any collection) — pick a field, a condition (contains, is exactly, does not contain, is empty or is not empty) and a value. The field list shows every field that collection has.
  • 🦽 Physical, 👁️ Visual, 🦻 Hearing (Visitors only) — show only visitors who need that kind of assistance. Pick more than one to find visitors who need all of them.
  • Clear all filters resets everything. While a filter is on, the count at the top shows X of Y records.
Field filter tips: write dates as 2026-11-19, or just 2026-11 with contains for a whole month. Tick-box fields are matched as yes or no. Useful examples:
  • Repair Items → outcome is empty — items with no outcome recorded
  • Repair Items → patResult is exactly Fail — items that failed their PAT test
  • Repairers → email is empty — repairers without an email address
The field filter resets when you choose a different collection, because each collection has different fields.

User Manager

Lists every user who has ever logged in. People waiting for approval are shown first — press Approve to let them see the repair records. Admins can also grant or revoke admin access here.

Finding someone: filters at the top of the list help when there are a lot of users:

  • Status buttons — All, ⏳ Waiting (not yet approved), ✅ Approved (approved volunteers) and 🛡 Admins. Each shows how many people it has.
  • Search — type part of a name or email address.
  • Last seen — people seen in the last 7, 30 or 90 days, or Not seen for 90+ days. Use that last one to find old accounts you may want to remove or delete.
  • Clear resets the filters. While a filter is on, the screen shows Showing X of Y users.

Deleting a user: press the red 🗑 button on their row, then Delete user to confirm. Use this when someone asks for their data to be deleted, or to remove an account that shouldn't be there. Deleting a user removes:

  • their Google sign-in for the app;
  • their profile (name, email, photo, last seen);
  • their approval, and admin access if they had it;
  • their entries in the activity log, so their sign-ins also disappear from Notifications.

Repair records are kept, including their name as a repairer on items they worked on, because these are the café's records. Their entry in Admin → Repairers is a separate record and is not deleted. Remove it there too if needed.

Deleting can't be undone. You can't delete yourself: there is no 🗑 button on your own row. If a deleted person signs in with Google again, they appear as a new user waiting for approval. To stop someone using the app without deleting their history, use Remove approval instead.

Notifications (bell icon)

Admins see a bell icon in the header. When someone new signs in and is waiting for approval:

  • a red number on the bell shows how many people are waiting;
  • if you are signed in at the time, a pop-up appears in the bottom-right corner a few seconds after they sign in — click it to review. It closes by itself after 15 seconds, or straight away once that person has been approved.
No false alarms: the pop-up only appears for someone who has just signed in and is still waiting a few seconds later, so you won't be asked to review a person who isn't there. If you delete a user who still has the app open on a device, they won't reappear as a nameless person waiting for approval.

Click the bell to see who is waiting and press Approve next to their name. They get in straight away, without needing to sign in again. Below that, Recent shows the last few people who signed in (👋) or were approved (✅). Use See all notifications → for the full list, or Open Admin → Users for more options.

On a phone, the list opens across the full width of the screen, just below the bell. If it is long, scroll inside the list to see the rest. Scrolling the page, or tapping anywhere outside the list, closes it.

Notifications page

The Notifications page lists everything, newest first and grouped by day (Today, Yesterday, then dates). Open it from the bell with See all notifications →. There are three kinds of notification:

TypeShows
⏳ Waiting for approvalSomeone has signed in but can't see the repair records yet. Highlighted in orange, with an Approve button.
✅ ApprovedWho was approved, who approved them and when
👋 Signed inEach time someone opens the app, with their device (for example iPhone · Safari) and the time

Filtering the list:

  • Type buttons along the top (All, Waiting for approval, Approved, Signed in) show how many of each there are; click one to show only that type. The Type drop-down does the same.
  • Person — type part of a name or email address; names are suggested as you type. You can also click anyone's name in the list to show only their notifications.
  • How old — Today, Last 7 days (the default), Last 30 days or Any time.
  • Clear filters puts everything back.

The page shows 50 notifications at a time; use Show older at the bottom to see more.

People waiting for approval always show, whatever the How old setting, because they still need dealing with. Sign-ins come from the activity log, which keeps 90 days, so older sign-ins are not listed.

Notification history

Notifications don't disappear once you've seen or dealt with them, so you can always look back at what has happened:

  • When someone is approved, their ⏳ Waiting for approval notification turns into an ✅ Approved one, showing who approved them and when. It stays in the list from then on.
  • Approvals go back to the very first person approved, including people approved before the notification history was added.
  • Sign-ins go back 90 days.

To look back, open the Notifications page and set How old to Any time. Choose a type, such as Approved, to narrow it down, and use Show older to keep going back. The bell drop-down only shows the most recent few under Recent.

If an admin removes someone's approval in Admin → Users, their Approved notification is removed too. If they sign in again, they show as Waiting for approval once more.

Activity

Admin → Activity shows who has been using the app, when, on what device and which parts they used. Only admins can see it, and entries are kept for 90 days.

  • People — a card for each person shows when they were last active, their device, how many days they have used the app and the part they use most. Click a card to show only that person; click it again (or show everyone) to go back.
  • Timeline — everything that happened, newest first and grouped by day: 🔓 sign-ins, 🔒 sign-outs, 📄 pages opened and changes such as ➕ registering, ✅ completing, 🗑️ deleting an item or 🤖 asking the AI. Click an item name to open it.

Filtering the activity:

  • Type buttons — Everything, Sign-ins, Changes made or Pages opened, each showing how many entries it has.
  • Search — type part of a name, email, page, item or device.
  • When — Today, Yesterday, Last 7 days, Last 30 days, or everything kept.
  • Part of the app — pick a page (for example Repair list or Ask AI) to see who has been opening it.
  • Device — for example iPhone · Safari or Windows PC · Chrome.
  • Clear resets all the filters, including the selected person. While a filter is on, the screen shows Showing X of Y entries.
Part of the app matches pages that were opened, so it shows visits to that page, not changes made there. Filters can be combined: for example, pick a person and Changes made with Last 7 days to see what they changed this week.

Backup & Restore

Admin → Backup & Restore saves the whole app to a spreadsheet, or puts data back from one.

  • ⬇️ Download — give the backup a name and press Download Backup. Every collection is saved to one Excel file, one sheet per collection.
  • ⬆️ Restore — choose a backup file, name the upload, tick the collections to restore and pick Replace (make them exactly match the backup) or Merge (overwrite matching records, keep everything else). Sensitive collections such as users and admins start unticked, and you can download a safety backup of the current data first.

Backup history lists every download, restore and Clear Data run, with who did it, when and how many records. To find an entry:

  • Type buttons — All, ⬇️ Downloads, ⬆️ Uploads (restores) or 🧹 Cleared, each showing how many entries it has.
  • Search — type part of the backup name or file name.
  • By — show only the backups done by one person.
  • When — Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Last 12 months or More than 12 months ago. For restores this is when the restore happened, not when the file was first downloaded.
  • Clear resets the filters. While a filter is on, the screen shows Showing X of Y entries; if nothing matches it says No backups match these filters.
Tip: to find the most recent backup before restoring, choose ⬇️ Downloads — the newest is at the top.

Version Manager

Displays the current application version and description. The version shown in the header and footer always reflects the latest deployed code automatically — no manual update needed after a deployment.

Tips

  • The version shown in the header updates automatically on every new deployment — you never need to set it manually.
  • Secondary repairers can be added or removed directly from the session dashboard without opening the full edit form.
  • Past session cards on the schedule screen show a mini donut chart so you can see at a glance how a session went.
  • The Issues badge count keeps open issues visible at all times without having to visit the tracker.
  • Status changes made in the session dashboard items list are saved to Firestore immediately — no separate save step required.
